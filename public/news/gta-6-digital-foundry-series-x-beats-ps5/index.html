Digital Foundry's big GTA 6 console comparison: Xbox Series X runs it better than the standard PS5

By Adrian R · November 25, 2026

Digital Foundry put GTA VI on all four consoles side by side. The PS5 Pro is still the best place to play, but the Xbox Series X beats the standard PS5 with fewer frame rate drops. Here's the full ranking.

At launch, Digital Foundry's first tests put the standard PS5 and the Xbox Series X roughly level. Its full four-console comparison, published this week, is more decisive: the Series X comes out ahead of the standard PS5. Push Square, TweakTown, GGRecon and Kotaku have all reported the findings.

The ranking

  1. PS5 Pro. Still the best version by some distance. It holds 30 fps almost perfectly, upscales a 1440p image with PSSR, and adds ray-traced shadows the standard PS5 doesn't get (TweakTown).
  2. Xbox Series X. Same 1440p resolution and matching settings to the standard PS5, but with noticeably fewer frame rate drops. It also supports 120Hz VRR, which smooths over the small dips that remain.
  3. PS5. Digital Foundry found it "suffers substantially more frequent frame-rate drops than Series X". The drops show up most when driving fast and in crowded scenes such as the airport.
  4. Xbox Series S. Last, as before: 720p upscaled to 1080p, often below 30 fps when driving, down into the teens in heavy fights, plus brief audio cut-outs.

Digital Foundry's Oliver Mackenzie called the Series X result "a little surprising". His explanation, as TweakTown reports it, is that both consoles use the same settings, and that asks more of the PS5's slightly smaller graphics chip. In the worst moments, GGRecon says both base consoles drop into the mid-to-upper 20s.

Does this change anything for you?

  • Every version is still capped at 30 fps. There's no 40 or 60 fps mode on any console, including the PS5 Pro.
  • If you're on Series X, turn on VRR if your TV supports 120Hz. It's the reason small drops are less noticeable there.
  • If you're on standard PS5, the difference is mostly in fast driving and dense crowds. It's not a reason to buy a second console, but it is the first area to watch if Rockstar patches performance.

No performance patch yet

A post claiming Rockstar had apologised for stuttering and would patch it on November 25 has been going around since November 20. PC Games Hardware and other fact-checks found no such announcement on Rockstar's Newswire or support site, and no patch arrived. The latest update is still 01.002.002, which didn't touch performance. Gizmodo also reports that some PS5 players are still getting crashes, including error CE-108255-1, after that patch; Rockstar and Sony haven't commented.
