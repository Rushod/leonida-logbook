GTA 6 reviews are in: a 99 on Metacritic, and the complaints worth knowing

By Adrian R · November 18, 2026

GTA VI's PS5 Metascore stood at 99 from 98 early reviews. What critics loved, what they didn't, and what Digital Foundry found about 30 fps.

Reviews for GTA VI went live on November 18, the day before launch, and the early numbers are about as high as they go. On the morning of November 18, the PS5 version held a 99 Metascore from 98 critic reviews. The Xbox Series X score was also 99, but from only nine reviews. Both will move as more reviews come in.

For comparison, GTA IV sits at 98 and GTA V at 97, so GTA VI is on course to be the best-reviewed game in the series.

Perfect scores so far

IGN, VICE, Push Square and Cultured Vultures all gave 10 out of 10, and Creative Bloq gave 5 stars.

What critics loved

  • Leonida itself. Reviewers describe a dense, handcrafted world full of detail and places to go.
  • Driving. IGN calls the handling the best in the series, with more weight and more convincing damage.
  • Jason and Lucia. The writing, the performances and their relationship get wide praise, as does a more grown-up story than past GTAs.
  • Robberies you plan yourself rather than follow a script, and smarter police. See our robberies guide and wanted level guide.

What they didn't

  • Controls. The Guardian says complicated button inputs can start fights you didn't mean to, and Kotaku calls some controls cumbersome.
  • Guns. GamingBolt finds the smaller firearms clunky, and The Verge found switching weapons in a fight frustrating.
  • Driving early on. GamingBible found the heavier handling hard at first, especially when crashes cost money to repair.
  • Pacing. Kotaku says the later chapters of the story drag.
  • No mission replays. IGN notes you can't replay finished story missions one at a time, which you could in GTA V.

Performance: 30 fps everywhere

There is no 60 fps mode on any console, including PS5 Pro. Push Square gave the game a 10 but still flagged that. Digital Foundry's tests found:

  • PS5 Pro: a "near flawless" 30 fps with perfect frame pacing over more than 50 hours. It only fell to 24 fps when the tester set off huge numbers of explosions on purpose.
  • PS5: small dips throughout, and longer drops below 30 in heavy action.
  • Xbox Series X|S: not tested yet.

Full details by console are in our frame rate guide.

Should the complaints change your mind?

Probably not, if you were already excited. Most of the criticism is about controls and feel, which you get used to. If smooth frame rates matter a lot to you, the 30 fps cap is the one thing that won't change with practice.
