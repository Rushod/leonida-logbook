Rockstar's GTA 6 developer notes: 1,000 NPC routines, months on one door, and a plea to skip the guides

By Adrian R · November 23, 2026

Rockstar gave press a packet of notes from more than 20 GTA VI developers. The highlights, including its design chief asking players to stop chasing completion percentages.

Alongside review copies, Rockstar sent press a set of notes in which named developers explain how GTA VI was made. InvenGlobal published a long summary on November 18, and Kotaku wrote about one part of it on November 21. These are Rockstar's own words, so this is about as official as behind-the-scenes detail gets.

"Stop thinking in tasks"

According to Kotaku, Stephen Donkin, Rockstar's vice president of design, says the team hopes players drop the habit of working through tasks, completion percentages and online guides, and instead "let the game unfold in front of them". Senior design director Ghyan Koehne talks about wanting players to feel a sense of wonder.

Yes, we see the irony of reporting that on a guide site. We agree with the spirit of it: on a first playthrough, wander off and don't worry about 100%. Our walkthrough and collectibles pages will be here when you're stuck or want to clean up later.

Other highlights

  • Background life: the team made about 1,000 sets of activity animations for the routines of people in the world.
  • Small things take time: gameplay animation director Mike Jones says the moment Lucia pushes a door open with her hip took months to get right.
  • Location scouting: John Macpherson, lead director of world and population design, describes the team scouting real Florida locations.
  • The look of the UI: the classic Pricedown font stays, and the phone is now held in the character's hand rather than floating on screen.
  • Music: Rockstar's music team describes 1980s Miami influences for the regions of Leonida and a collaboration with French electronic duo Justice.
  • Cars: physics technology director Ben Lyons says team members raced in the 24 Hours of Nürburgring after GTA V and took a class win.
  • Sound: the audio team recorded in an anechoic chamber to get speakers and other sounds right.

The notes also include a foreword from Rockstar founder Sam Houser. Neither summary says anything about a PC version or an online mode, which Rockstar still hasn't announced.
